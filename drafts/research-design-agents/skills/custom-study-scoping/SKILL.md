# Custom study scoping

A custom study is designed for one client's decision. Syndicated research is
produced once and sold to many. Scoping decides what the client's decision
needs that syndicated and secondary sources cannot supply, and designs only that.

Start from a research brief (the `research-brief` skill). The client's
decision owner and the decision itself must be named. If they are not, ask
before writing the plan.

## 1. Business problem to research problem

- The business decision, in the client's words.
- The research problem: what must be known, and how sure, for that decision.
- What the client already knows or believes (record beliefs as hypotheses,
  not facts).
- What would change the decision. If nothing would, say the study is not needed.

## 2. Build, buy or reuse

Before designing new fieldwork, check and record in the evidence ledger:

1. The client's own data (CRM, product analytics, support tickets, past studies).
2. Public primary sources (official statistics, filings, regulators).
3. Syndicated reports. Record the publisher, date and price as listed, and
   that the contents were not reviewed unless they were.

Recommend reuse where it answers the question. New fieldwork covers only the gap.

## 3. Study architecture

Phases in order, each with its objective, method (use the
`primary-research-design` skill for any fieldwork phase), sample, output and
the decision gate that releases the next phase. A typical shape: desk review,
then qualitative exploration, then quantitative sizing or testing. Drop phases
the decision does not need.

## 4. Deliverables and acceptance

For each deliverable: format, contents, the question it answers, and how the
client will know it is complete. Include the evidence ledger as a deliverable
so the client can audit the findings.

## 5. Assumptions, exclusions, dependencies

- Assumptions, each one that would change price or timeline marked.
- Exclusions: questions, markets or methods outside scope.
- Client dependencies: data access, customer lists, approvals, and the date
  each is needed.
- Data and access constraints. An advertised dataset or API is not access
  until the project is authorized and has tested it.

## 6. Risks and reviews

- Method risks (low incidence, panel quality, small segments).
- Regulated topics: health, finance, children, employment, personal data.
  Name the specialist review needed (privacy, legal, clinical, security) and
  the questions for it. Never state that the study is compliant.
- Reviewers and approval gates before fieldwork and before delivery.

## 7. Output

Proposal sections: background and decision; objectives; reuse findings;
study architecture; sample and method per phase; deliverables; timeline;
assumptions and exclusions; risks and reviews; decisions for the client.
Timelines and budgets are estimates with their drivers shown.
